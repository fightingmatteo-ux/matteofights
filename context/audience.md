# @matteofights — Audience

_Read for hook psychology, targeting, and copy that lands._

## Who's watching
Fighters and aspiring fighters. Trains 4–5x a week — Muay Thai or boxing, maybe some BJJ. Has a job
or studies. Bangkok, London, Rome, Sydney — location doesn't matter, the identity does. Gets up for
roadwork when he has to (sometimes doesn't). Knows what to do, doesn't always do it, keeps
restarting. Scrolls IG after a session instead of sleeping.

He's **not looking for motivation** — he's looking for **proof that others do the work**, and for
tactical edges he can use. When he finds someone who's clearly lived it, he goes deep.

## Core frustrations (use in hooks)
1. Can't train as much as he wants — schedule, money, or injury caps his volume.
2. No one around him trains seriously; his environment limits his ceiling.
3. Progress feels invisible — working but can't see the compounding.
4. Generic fitness content doesn't speak to a fighter specifically.

## Why the viral reel worked
The 160k reel ("8 ways to fight like a killer without sparring 5x/week") hit the exact nerve:
**how to get dangerous when you can't put in unlimited gym time.** That curiosity gap —
aspirational outcome WITHOUT the expected requirement — is the audience's central tension. Mine it.

## Language they use
"stay dangerous" / "the work" / "roadwork" / "rounds" / "sparring partner" / "camp" / "the pocket"
/ "conditioning" / "discipline" / "no excuses" / "show up".

## Who it's NOT for
People who want motivation without execution. Validation-seekers. Casual gym-goers who train twice
a week and call it a lifestyle.

## The conversion mechanism
High-intent viewers are funneled to a DM ("DM me 'FIGHT'…") for help building a training schedule —
the seed of a coaching/lead-gen motion. Secondary to growth for now, but it's the path to revenue.
