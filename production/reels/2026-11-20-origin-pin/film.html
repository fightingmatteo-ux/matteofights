Origin pin · film at home · one take

School came easy. Fighting didn't.

Press record once and read top to bottom. The story comes first, then five loop endings, each with its opening built in. The openings look different because of the footage the editor puts on them, so you only need one camera setup. Pause where it says PAUSE. If you trip, stop, breathe, and start that sentence again without stopping the recording.

Before you press record

How to deliver it

Look into the lens, not the screen. Loop endings a notch more energy than feels natural, said in one breath with no pause at the ✂. After each one, hold eye contact for 2 seconds in silence. Smile on the appendicitis line. Talk the story to one friend; the bold red words are the ones to hit.

Record

How the loop works

Every version ends on a sentence that runs straight into its opening. You say it in one breath: close, bridge, opening. The editor cuts at the ✂, moves the opening (red) to the very start of the video and leaves the bridge at the end. When the reel replays, the bridge flows into the opening and nobody can tell it restarted. So you don't record the openings on their own: they live inside Part 2.

Part 1 · The story (same in every version)
1 · Where it started

“I started with jiu-jitsu at ten. Three years on the mat. Then a street fight showed me the mat isn't the street. So I switched to striking.”

2 · School

“Back then, school came easy. I never studied, and I still passed. I'd never had to work for anything.”

3 · The first fight

“Then I booked my first Muay Thai fight. I was sixteen, and I was scared. Scared of getting knocked out in front of all my friends. So if I hadn't trained that day, I couldn't sleep.”

4 · The twist (smile)

“The day before the fight, I got appendicitis. Fight cancelled. Pushed back three months, at a different stadium. So I trained three more months.”

5 · What fighting is to me (your words)

“That's what fighting is to me. It's the first thing that taught me: if you want an outcome, you have to work for it. Consistency. Discipline. How to actually get good at something.”

Say it your way. Keep the red line close to word for word.

6 · Since

“Since then, five countries. Thailand, Switzerland, Italy, Morocco, Brazil.”

Count them on your fingers.

7 · What's kept fight two away

“Then I moved to Bangkok. Boxer's fracture. And this March, the day before I went back to school, an accident in Koh Samui. Broken fibula. Torn ACL.”

Say only “an accident”. Never a car, a crash or driving.

8 · Now

“I can't jump. I can't spar. But I can do this.”

Stand up, step back, shadowbox for 20 s, sharp and relaxed (no jumps, no explosive kicks). Step back in and sit.

“Same rule as that first fight. You want it, you work for it.”

Pause 3 s, reset. Keep recording

Part 2 · The five loop endings (one breath each)
AResult

“Follow if you want to see the comeback. Because right now it's still this. ✂ One fight at sixteen. I'm nineteen, and I still haven't had my second.”

Opens tight on your face with “1ST FIGHT: 16 / 2ND FIGHT: ?”. The editor punches in on the bridge too, so the replay is seamless.

Pause 2 s, look at the lens

BContrast

“Follow if you want to see the comeback. And honestly? ✂ School came easy to me. Fighting was the first thing that didn't.”

Opens on your beginner pad kick under your voice, cuts to you at 2 s.

Pause 2 s, look at the lens

CStory peak

“Follow if you want to see the comeback. Because this isn't the first time. ✂ The day before my first fight, I ended up in hospital.”

Split screen: you on top, first-fight walkout below.

Pause 5 s, silent, look at the lens (top half of the split)

DInjury

“Follow if you want to see the comeback. Because everything changed in March. ✂ One day before I went back to school, an accident took out my knee. Broken fibula. Torn ACL.”

Opens on the X-ray under your voice, then you.

Pause 2 s, look at the lens

EReframe

“Follow if you want to see the comeback. And I know what you're thinking. ✂ People think fighting is just violence. For me it's the only thing that ever taught me discipline.”

Opens on your sharpest shadowboxing clip under your voice, then you.
