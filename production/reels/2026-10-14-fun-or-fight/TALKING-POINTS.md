# Talking points: Fun or fight

Film handheld at eye level, chin clear of the bottom edge. Say each point 2–3 times in your own words,
pause a beat between takes. Only the hooks are word-for-word. Target ~30 s. Talk like you're settling an
argument with a friend, not teaching a class. Every point turns on BUT or SO, never "and also".

Built on evidence: "Some people train for fun." = best hook on the account (7,413 likes + comments);
identity/contrast beats instruction ~7x. Variants are three different ANGLES, not three wordings
(same-shape variants all capped at ~2k).

**Hook (verbatim, 3 versions for the A/B/C test):**
- A (contrast, the proven shape): "Some people train for fun. That's why they freeze when it's real."
- B (provocation): "You're not training. You're exercising."
- C (story): "My first fight was his sixth. That's when I learned the difference."

**Credibility beat:** first Muay Thai fight at 16, his 6th fight, your 1st; trained in 5 countries
(Zurich, Essaouira, São Paulo, Koh Samui, Rome). Source: context/founder.md.

**Your points (free talk):**
1. Most people go to the gym to sweat. Nothing wrong with that. BUT there's nobody in front of them,
   they're just doing the motions.
2. A fight is different: someone is trying to make you fail. My first fight at 16, his sixth, my first.
   I found that out in round one. SO it changed how I train.
3. Training to fight = there's always a person in front of you. Shadowboxing: you picture someone.
   Pads: you react, you don't memorise. Sparring: you go in scared and learn to think while scared.
4. The take (say it flat, no smile): "If training never scares you, you're exercising. That's fine.
   Just don't call it fighting."

**Your example (one real moment):** the first fight at 16. Keep it to one line; don't retell the KO/draw
(that's the pinned origin reel).
