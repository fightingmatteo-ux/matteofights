## What the winners do (and what it means for you)

1. **Something is already happening in frame 0.** A fight squaring up (kennkihiu), a fight mid-swing (djhamp,
   Bas), a kid shadowboxing (brezojr), a bloody fighter by 4 s (Soo Hoo). A plain head with nothing else only wins
   when the line itself is the event (Santino, James Harris). For you: put a fight or training clip in the first
   second (split screen or a cut at 1 to 2 s). That's what makes it look different on mute.
2. **A text board that tells the whole story in one line, on screen from 0 s and held 5 s+.** It's packed with
   numbers and says nothing about a lesson: `51. 27 years marriage, gone baby`, `"I will start MMA" 2017`,
   `70kg`. The board doesn't repeat the spoken line, it adds to it.
3. **Spoken line 1 is the turning point or someone else's quote, never a hello.** "I was married for 27 years."
   "Last week, a woman asked me..." Under 15 words, a number or a name in it, the why left open. Same as your own
   tests: result and contrast openings win (75% / 68% placing), story-from-the-beginning loses (25%).
4. **Proof shows up as footage, not as a claim.** James Harris shows the school ID and the baby photo at 14 s;
   Brezo shows 2017 then 2026. Your proof is the tape: 5 countries, first fight at 16, the knee.
5. **Niche winners are 15 to 25 s, story winners 50 to 90 s.** A pin can run long if every 2 to 3 s brings a new
   picture (Brezo: 9 cuts in 22 s; James Harris holds a selfie but flashes photos at the turning points).

Niche-wide (Buzzfy, 146 reels): 53% of the top reels open on a picture with no hook line (`visual_only`); among
smaller accounts, `pov`, `list_number` and `bold_claim` openings have the highest median plays (1.3M / 952k /
768k), `how_to` the lowest (29k). Almost none ask for anything (96% no CTA).
