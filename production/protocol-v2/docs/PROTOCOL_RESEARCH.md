# THE PROTOCOL — Research Synthesis

Condensed from 7 parallel research passes (~1,100 lines, ~90 web sources) conducted for THE PROTOCOL, a free at-home fighter performance system for @matteofights. Full detail, every source, and every evidence grade lives in the individual reports under `research/`:

1. `01-combat-sc.md` — Combat sports strength & conditioning

6. `06-mobile-ux.md` — Mobile fitness UX

This document pulls out only what changes the product. Evidence grades below: **[STRONG]** meta-analysis/multiple RCTs · **[MODERATE]** consistent primary studies · **[WEAK]** limited/conflicting · **[PRACTICE-BASED]** expert consensus, no controlled trials.

## 1. The training science

### Legs and trunk throw the punch, not the arm
Punch force splits roughly 39% legs, 37% trunk, 24% arm, sequenced ankle→knee→hip→trunk→arm [MODERATE]. Upper-body maximal strength does **not** differentiate hard punchers from soft ones in elite boxers — lower-body maximal and explosive strength does [MODERATE]. Kicking power is hip/pelvis-rotation-driven, not limb-speed-driven [MODERATE]. This directly overturns the "big bench = big punch" belief and is the single most useful reframe available to the product — Strength category copy should say so explicitly.

### Fatigue attacks force and repeatability first, technique last
Ordered by evidence strength: strike force/velocity degrades first → repeatable output volume falls → reaction time/consistency slips → decision-making is the most fatigue-vulnerable in theory but weakly evidenced directly [WEAK-MODERATE]. Rehearsed technique itself may hold up better than assumed — one MMA study found no skill decline despite fatigue [WEAK, single study, worth flagging]. Rear-leg drive — the biggest single contributor to strike force — is also the first thing fatigue erodes, a compounding weakness worth naming in copy.

### The four target sports converge more than they diverge
Boxing, Muay Thai, kickboxing and MMA share the same underlying signature: sustained heart rate near max, blood lactate 9–20+ mmol/L, VO2max needs 40–60 ml/kg/min scaling down with weight class, and short-burst/incomplete-recovery work:rest patterns throughout (exact ratios differ — boxing ~18:1 elite collapsing to 6:1 in fatigued novices; Muay Thai ~2:3; MMA 1:2–1:4) [MODERATE-STRONG]. This licenses one shared foundation instead of four separate programs. The real divergences are narrow: kicking-sport hip/pelvis ROM and shin/ankle tolerance (Muay Thai/kickboxing/MMA, absent in boxing); clinch isometric neck/grip/hip-drive endurance (Muay Thai/MMA, absent in boxing/point-kickboxing); MMA's added ground-grappling repeat-effort and grip demand. **Decision: build one shared core, with two small labeled branches (kicking, clinch) a boxer can skip without loss.**

### What's reliably underdeveloped in non-elite fighters
Hip mobility/abduction strength, rear-leg force contribution to striking, neck strength (rarely trained despite strong concussion-protective evidence, and structurally required in the two sports with clinch), aerobic-plus-repeat-effort capacity (vs. generic steady-state cardio), core rotational strength (a 6-week isolated core program alone produced a 24% increase in rear-hand punch power in trained boxers [MODERATE-STRONG]), grip endurance, shoulder/rotator-cuff resilience (amateur boxers show ~2x the shoulder-injury rate of pros).

### Conditioning: HIIT/repeat-effort is the best-evidenced modality in the whole review
Two independent systematic reviews with meta-analysis (Franchini 2019, Vasconcelos 2020) converge on HIIT reliably improving aerobic capacity, repeat-effort ability, and combat-specific power in martial artists [MODERATE-STRONG] — the strongest evidence base of any single training modality surveyed across all seven reports. Aerobic base is explicitly the *recovery-between-bursts* system, not the peak-output system — boxers' oxygen uptake climbs from ~85% VO2max in round 1 toward ~100% by the final round [STRONG for the physiology]. Build both: aerobic base as the foundation, HIIT/repeat-effort intervals as the primary conditioning tool.

### Plyometrics and mobility: real, but the product must stay claim-conservative
The only combat-sport plyometric meta-analysis available is dominated by fencing/taekwondo (9 of 12 studies) — striking-sport-specific transfer is only trivial-to-small (Hedges g 0.35–0.72) [MODERATE for the general combat population studied, WEAK specifically for boxing/Muay Thai/kickboxing/MMA]. No combat-specific mobility intervention RCTs were found anywhere in this research — that category rests entirely on injury-epidemiology and anatomical reasoning [PRACTICE-BASED]. **Decision: include both, dose plyometrics by foot-contact volume (evidence-backed), never promise a quantified performance-transfer number, and frame mobility as sound practice rather than an evidence-backed guarantee.**

### Concurrent training: the "cardio kills gains" belief is outdated
Large recent meta-analyses (Schumann, 43 studies) show minimal interference for hypertrophy/strength under realistic frequencies; the one place interference is real and consistent is power/explosive adaptations specifically [STRONG-MODERATE]. Practical rule: sequence strength before conditioning within a session; separate power/ballistic S&C from hard combat sessions by ≥3h same-day, 6h+ preferred.

### At-home training is not a compromise product — with one honest ceiling
Hypertrophy is load-independent [STRONG]; training to failure isn't required [STRONG]; matched-effort calisthenics and gym training produce statistically similar strength/hypertrophy gains [MODERATE-STRONG]. Isometrics are a genuinely underused, well-evidenced substitute for external load — overcoming isometrics build RFD, long-duration yielding holds (≥30s, ≥70% effort) build tendon stiffness [STRONG]. Unilateral work (split squats, Nordics, step-ups) isn't "half of bilateral" — it has its own specific transfer to a fighter's actual one-leg-loaded movement patterns [MODERATE-STRONG]. Plyometric dosing (80–120 foot contacts/session, 1–2x/week) translates directly into apartment-safe programming with low-impact substitutions.

**The one place bodyweight training genuinely plateaus:** maximal absolute strength and heavy posterior-chain hypertrophy have a real, physics-based ceiling — a barbell loads past 200% bodyweight, unilateral bodyweight movements top out near 100% [STRONG]. The product should say this plainly and offer a ranked, evidence-backed equipment upgrade path rather than pretending the ceiling doesn't exist: skipping rope → resistance bands → pull-up bar (the one bodyweight-strength gap with no substitute — vertical pulling) → suspension trainer → one adjustable kettlebell/dumbbell (highest-leverage single purchase) → weighted vest (optional/later).

### Programming: one rule, kept simple on purpose
**Double progression** — when the top of the rep range is hit for all sets with 1–2 reps left, add reps to the top of the range; once there, add difficulty (harder variation, unilateral shift, reduced rest, tempo) and drop back to the bottom of the range. Chosen over RPE-driven prescription because novice reps-in-reserve accuracy is measurably unreliable [MODERATE-WEAK] — rep-counting is a task beginners are already accurate at; numeric effort-rating is not. Minimum effective volume is real: 1–3 hard sets per movement, 2–3x/week, taken near failure, produces meaningful strength gains even in trained lifters [STRONG] — this matters because this athlete's recovery budget is already spent on 3–6 combat sessions/week. Volume is not the lever to pull.

**Sparring is the highest-stakes scheduling constraint in the system.** Treat hard sparring like an eccentric/plyometric bout: avoid power and heavy strength work for at least 24h after (48–72h ideal for genuinely hard sparring), and don't place power work in the 3–6h immediately before sparring either [MODERATE, extrapolated from general eccentric-exercise literature — direct sparring-recovery dosing studies are sparse].

**Monitoring stays cheap and simple.** Post-session RPE × duration (sRPE) plus a 3-question daily wellness check (sleep, soreness, fatigue) is the defensible minimum [STRONG for sRPE validity]. Explicitly **not** building Acute:Chronic Workload Ratio into v1 — its predictive validity is actively disputed and it needs 4 weeks of data before it's useful.

**No hardcoded deload.** No direct evidence exists for whether this exact population (already combat-training 3–6x/week, doing minimal-dose S&C on top) needs a scheduled deload — treat "no scheduled deload" as a working assumption and let the wellness/sRPE signal trigger a reactive backoff instead.

## 2. Trust, psychology, and the free/paid line

### The assessment beats the PDF — this is the strongest lever in the whole research set
Interactive diagnostics substantially outperform static content on conversion, completion, and perceived lead quality across every source found [B/C grade — practitioner-benchmark strength, but the largest, most consistent effect in the entire research pass]. **Decision: THE PROTOCOL leads with a short fighter-performance self-assessment, not a downloadable guide.**

### Effort only pays off if it's completed
The IKEA effect (effort → higher valuation) requires *successful* completion — an abandoned assessment produces no trust benefit and just adds friction [B]. Keep the assessment completable in 3–5 minutes, matching the PLG time-to-value benchmark where 7-day activation strongly predicts 3-month retention [A].

### Avoid Barnum results
Vague, universally-flattering feedback rates as "insightful" regardless of accuracy [A — this is a real, well-documented risk, not hypothetical]. Every assessment result must trace to a specific input the user gave, and include at least one honest, non-flattering point — not uniform praise.

### Email capture: one field, instant delivery
Conversion degrades with every additional form field, steeply past 5 [A — the most quantifiable finding in this whole research pass]. Asking for an Instagram handle or phone number at first capture is separately documented as a conversion-killer. **Decision: email only at first capture; deliver instantly on-page; collect anything else later, once trust is established.** This is a direct, evidence-based change from the current `gate.html`, which asks for both email and Instagram handle before showing any content — see Spec §12 for the reworked flow.

### Progress, not streaks
Streak/loss-aversion mechanics move short-term engagement but carry documented "guilt churn" risk — users who miss a day under a rigid streak are more likely to quit entirely than users on flexible, plan-based tracking [D grade — evidence is mixed but consistently tilts this direction]. THE PROTOCOL is a finite, six-session system, not an infinite habit loop — progress-toward-completion framing ("3 of 6") fits the content shape; streaks don't.

### Other well-evidenced, cheap wins
**Endowed progress** — credit the assessment itself as step 1, don't start the user at zero [A, Nunes & Drèze]. **Implementation intentions** — prompt a specific if-then commitment at setup ("when I finish work on [days], I'll do the 20-minute session") — one of the best-evidenced, lowest-cost behavior-change tools available [A]. **Fresh-start framing** — Mondays, month starts, "new chapter" language reliably increase aspirational follow-through [A]. **Pratfall effect** — one small, specific admitted limitation, stated *after* competence is shown (post-assessment, not before), builds trust; stated too early or too broadly, it backfires [B].

### The free/paid line has no formula — but both failure modes are real
Give too little and the free product reads as low-trust content; give too much and there's no reason to want the paid product or STANCE [B, well-documented, symmetric failure modes]. Applied heuristic: give away a genuinely complete *system* (the assessment, the six sessions, the mechanism/why) — that's what earns attention and trust. Reserve *ongoing, adaptive, and social* value (deeper personalization over time, live coaching, STANCE's network effects) for what comes next. The free product should feel complete on its own terms, not a truncated preview.

### Ethical lines (already ruled out by the brief, restated because the research independently arrives at the same list)
No fake scarcity, no fabricated testimonials, no confirmshaming, no forced continuity, no deliberately crippled free tier, no Barnum-style fake personalization, no anxiety-driven streak mechanics.

## 3. Mobile UX — hard technical constraints the build must respect

These aren't style preferences; they're platform behavior the implementation has to be built around from the start.

- **Never trust a live-incrementing timer.** Backgrounded/inactive browser tabs throttle `setInterval`/`setTimeout` to roughly once a minute. Store an absolute end-timestamp (`Date.now() + duration`) and recompute remaining time from it on every tick/resume — this is the standard, non-negotiable workaround.
- **Wake Lock is not reliable.** Supported in Safari 16.4+ in-browser; historically broken inside home-screen-installed web apps (WebKit bug 254545), reportedly fixed iOS 18.4 but unverified against Apple's own release notes. Feature-detect, request on start, re-acquire on `visibilitychange`, and design the timer so it stays correct even if the lock silently fails.
- **localStorage is not permanent on iOS Safari.** ITP caps script-writable storage at 7 days of site inactivity (resets on each visit — low risk for an active user, real risk for someone who does the assessment and doesn't return for over a week). Home-screen-installed apps are exempt. Save state on every interaction; be honest in copy that persistence isn't guaranteed rather than promising it.
- **No push notifications without manual install.** iOS Safari only allows Web Push for home-screen-installed apps, and there's no install *prompt* on iOS — it's Share → Add to Home Screen, manually. This can only ever be a light, earned suggestion after a good first session, never a gate.
- **Load speed is the single biggest lever.** 53% of mobile visits abandon past 3 seconds; a cold Instagram-referral visitor has the least patience of any visitor this product will see. Target LCP < 2.5s, JS < 150KB gzipped. This argues against a heavy JS framework for what is fundamentally a content-and-timer app.
- **One-exercise-at-a-time focus mode, persistent auto-starting/manually-skippable rest timer, audio+haptic transition cues** (phone is often face-down or on the floor), **large bottom-anchored touch targets** (44px practical floor for sweaty-fingers/fatigued-grip use) are the ranked, evidence-backed UX priorities, in that order.

## 4. Competitive positioning

### "No equipment, works anywhere" alone is not a differentiator
It's the standard line for FightCamp, Skillr, Shadow Boxing App, and MyCombat alike — commodity copy, not a hook. Two direct competitors (**FightTrainer**, "the performance platform for combat athletes... built by fighters, for fighters," $20/mo; **Fight Plan Pro**, "fighter-inspired fitness & performance system") already occupy close to the exact positioning drafted in the original brief, in near-identical language.

### The opening: neither direct competitor has a visible founder
Both FightTrainer and Fight Plan Pro market with no disclosed founder or credentials — an anonymous-brand pattern shared by nearly the entire no-equipment-app tier (MyCombat, Skillr, BXNG, PunchIQ, etc.). The credentialed tier that *does* earn trust in this space (Boxing Science, Phil Daru, Evolve University, Athletic Adaptations) does so with real, verifiable sport-science or fight backgrounds — vague "sports science" language without data is a red flag to this audience, not a credibility boost. The sharpest signal found: Sean Strickland publicly dismissed Francis Ngannou's trainer — an actual former pro kickboxer — as a "TikTok coach," because the public packaging read as influencer-coded. Combat-sports audiences discount even real credentials that are packaged wrong.

### Recommended positioning: transparent, in-progress legitimacy — with practicality as proof, not the headline
"Built in public by someone earning it, not claiming it." This is a direct fit with the existing @matteofights brand voice (`founder.md`: "not a guru, not a hype guy"; the injury→pivot→build narrative already the account's strongest content). It converts the liability the brief itself names — an unknown creator giving away training advice — into the actual content, and it's the one asset FightTrainer, Fight Plan Pro, and the anonymous-app tier structurally cannot copy: a real, named, audience-building creator. "No equipment, practical, works anywhere" still belongs in the product — as supporting proof of the honesty claim, not the lead message.

### Real complaints worth designing against
FightCamp: near-impossible cancellation, billing disputes, unreliable trackers. Everlast app: broken login/account system. Boxx: "a cardio app with a boxing theme" — no real technique progression. Cheap shadowboxing/combo apps: real skill content paywalled, glitchy, impractical combos. **Pattern across the whole low-cost tier: apps that are timers with combo lists, not real technique/programming depth.** THE PROTOCOL's coaching-cue-and-mechanism layer (see Spec §6) is a direct answer to this gap.

### Naming risk — flagged, not acted on
"THE PROTOCOL" is not clean: a real, active gym is named **The Protocol Strength & Conditioning** (Tucson, AZ), and Andrew Huberman's **"Foundational Fitness Protocol"** has made "Protocol" strongly associated with longevity/biohacking content in the broader fitness audience's mind. No direct combat-sports competitor uses the exact name. This is flagged for the record — the name is fixed by the brief and by existing brand assets already live at `matteofights.me/the-protocol.html`, so it is being kept, with the risk noted rather than acted on. The existing subtitle ("The At-Home Fighter Performance System") already does real disambiguation work and should stay attached to the name wherever it appears.

## 5. Key decisions this research settles

| Question | Decision | Evidence basis |
|---|---|---|
| One program or four sport-specific programs? | One shared core + two small labeled branches (kicking, clinch) | Physiological convergence across boxing/Muay Thai/kickboxing/MMA [MODERATE-STRONG] |
| Lead with a PDF or an interactive assessment? | Interactive assessment | Largest, most consistent effect in the trust research [B/C, but wide margin] |
| Gate content behind email immediately, or deliver value first? | Deliver assessment + results first; single-email capture to save/unlock the full system | Form-friction and instant-access research [A] |
| Ask for Instagram handle at signup? | No — collect later if needed for STANCE | Documented conversion-killer [A] |
| Streaks or progress bars? | Progress-toward-completion ("3 of 6") | Streak guilt-churn risk on a finite program [D, but consistent across sources] |
| RPE-based or rep-based progression? | Double progression (rep-counting) | Novice RPE/RIR accuracy is unreliable [MODERATE-WEAK] |
| Build ACWR-style load monitoring? | No — sRPE + 3-question wellness check only | ACWR predictive validity disputed; needs 4 weeks of data to be useful |
| How to handle the bodyweight strength ceiling? | State it plainly; offer a ranked equipment upgrade path | Physics-based ceiling is real and well-evidenced [STRONG] |
| Heavy JS framework or lean build? | Lean vanilla build, no framework runtime | Load-speed cliff is the single biggest completion risk found |
| Lead with "no equipment" or with creator credibility? | Lead with transparent, in-progress credibility; practicality as supporting proof | "No equipment" is commodity copy; visible founder is the one asset competitors lack |
| Rename away from "Protocol" given Huberman/gym-name collision? | Keep the name | Fixed by brief + existing live brand assets; risk flagged, not acted on |

## 6. Where the evidence is thin — carried into the Spec as explicit product caution

- No combat-specific mobility-intervention RCTs exist anywhere in this research. Mobility claims must stay in "sound practice" language, never "proven to reduce injury."
- Plyometric transfer to striking-sport performance specifically (not fencing/taekwondo) is only trivial-to-small and thinly evidenced. Never promise a quantified power gain from plyometric work.
- Direct sparring-recovery dosing studies are sparse; the 24h-minimum/48–72h-ideal buffer is a reasonable synthesis from general eccentric-exercise literature, not a directly-measured number.
- MMA-specific injury epidemiology is thinner than boxing's; much of the MMA injury-region data leans on judo/BJJ/wrestling as structural proxies.
- Deload necessity at this exact training volume has no direct evidence either way.
- Most lead-magnet/CRO conversion figures come from marketing-industry aggregation, not peer review — directionally trustworthy, not precise to the decimal.
- Wake Lock behavior inside iOS home-screen-installed apps as of iOS 18.4 was not verified against Apple's own release notes — treat as probable, build defensively regardless.
