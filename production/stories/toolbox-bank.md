# The Toolbox: story tool bank (draft 1, 19 tools)

Daily story, 3 frames: the moment, the fix, why it works (+ small source). Matteo crosses out anything he
wouldn't say. Status: draft, not approved. Citations checked 2026-10-29; old #19 (count reps) cut, its source didn't support it.

| # | The moment | The fix | Why it works | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | You freeze in the first 30 seconds of sparring | 2 short nose inhales, 1 long mouth exhale. Do it for 5 minutes a day, and a few rounds before the bell. | 5 minutes a day of long-exhale breathing improved mood and slowed breathing more than meditation did. | Balban et al., Cell Reports Medicine, 2023 |
| 2 | Nerves before a fight or hard sparring | Say "I'm excited," not "calm down." | Nerves and excitement are the same body state. Calming down fights it, relabelling uses it. | Brooks, J. of Experimental Psychology: General, 2014 |
| 3 | Your head is full of noise mid-round | Pick one word for the round ("jab", "feet") and think only that. | Short instructional cues beat open thinking for skill work. | Hatzigeorgiadis et al., Perspectives on Psych. Science, 2011 |
| 4 | Your technique gets stiff when you overthink it | Focus on the target, not your body. "Through the bag," not "rotate the hip." | Outside focus keeps movement automatic. Inside focus jams it. | Wulf, Int. Review of Sport & Exercise Psych., 2013 |
| 5 | You keep making the same mistake in sparring | Write one if-then: "If I get pressed to the ropes, then I pivot left." | If-then plans make the right move fire without thinking. | Gollwitzer & Sheeran, Adv. Experimental Social Psych., 2006 |
| 6 | Every session starts differently and so does your head | Same 3 steps before every round: tap gloves, breathe out, cue word. | A fixed routine shuts the door on doubt before you start. | Cotterill, Int. Review of Sport & Exercise Psych., 2010 |
| 7 | You can't train today (injury, travel) | 5 minutes: picture a round in first person, in real time, with the sounds and the gloves. | Detailed imagery trains the same pathways as doing it. | Holmes & Collins, J. of Applied Sport Psych., 2001 |
| 8 | Last round, your body wants to quit | Have 2 lines ready: "Keep your hands up." "One more exchange." | Rehearsed self-talk made people last longer at the same effort. | Blanchfield et al., Med. & Science in Sports & Exercise, 2014 |
| 9 | Panic talk in your head | Talk to yourself by name: "You've got this, Matteo," not "I." | Using your name puts distance between you and the stress. | Kross et al., J. of Personality & Social Psych., 2014 |
| 10 | Can't stop worrying before fight night | Write down the worries for 10 minutes, then close the notebook. | Students who wrote out their worries before a big exam stopped choking on it. | Ramirez & Beilock, Science, 2011 |
| 11 | Fight week and you can't fall asleep | Before bed, write tomorrow's to-do list. | People who wrote a to-do list fell asleep faster. | Scullin et al., J. of Experimental Psych.: General, 2018 |
| 12 | You train hard but sleep 6 hours | Aim for 10 hours in bed for a few weeks. | College players who did this sprinted faster and shot about 9% better. Small study (11 players). | Mah et al., Sleep, 2011 |
| 13 | You got dropped in sparring and want to quit | Talk to yourself like you'd talk to a teammate it happened to. | Being kind to yourself after a failure made people work harder to improve. | Breines & Chen, Personality & Social Psych. Bulletin, 2012 |
| 14 | Late rounds feel like a wall | Relax your face. A loose smile, not a grimace. | Runners used less oxygen smiling than frowning. (Just relaxing didn't help.) | Brick et al., Psych. of Sport & Exercise, 2018 |
| 15 | You choke when it matters | Right-handed? Squeeze a ball in your left hand for 30 seconds before. | Left-hand squeezes cut choking in penalty kicks, taekwondo kicks and badminton serves. Later studies are mixed. | Beckmann et al., J. of Experimental Psych.: General, 2013 |
| 16 | You only think about winning | Set goals on what you control: "Jab first in every exchange." | Process goals beat outcome goals for performance and anxiety. | Kingston & Hardy, The Sport Psychologist, 1997 |
| 17 | You scroll for an hour before hard sparring | No heavy screen time in the hour before. | Mental fatigue made the same workout feel harder and people quit earlier. | Marcora et al., J. of Applied Physiology, 2009 |
| 18 | You feel something bad before a fight and don't know what | Name it in one word: "scared", "angry", "tired". | Putting a feeling into words turns down the brain's alarm. | Lieberman et al., Psychological Science, 2007 |
| 19 | You leave every session thinking only about what went wrong | After training: 1 thing that worked, 1 thing to fix next time. Nothing more. | Experts improve by working on one specific weakness at a time, with feedback. | Ericsson et al., Psychological Review, 1993 |
