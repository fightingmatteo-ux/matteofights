## Why it works (and why the origin story didn't)
- **The story is evidence, not the subject.** The reel is about *the viewer's* brain. His loss is the proof.
  Our origin story was about Matteo, and retention fell to ~40% by 0:10 because the viewer had no stake yet.
- **Stakes in the first 4 s.** "Brain damage" and "for free" make it about you right away and cost you nothing to
  keep watching. The skip-proofing is the accusation, not curiosity alone.
- **One vulnerable detail** (punching the steering wheel) makes it personal in a single line. The story is still short.
- **The turn makes it bigger:** the fight is the *best* case, your normal Tuesday is the real problem.
- **Facts + deliverable = comments.** The same Karst pattern: a free, specific thing behind a keyword. 220 comments.

## How to use it for @matteofights
Formula: **accusation about the viewer (≤4 s) → contrast → Matteo's story as proof (≤15 s, one raw detail) → turn back
to the viewer's normal day → one verifiable fact → payoff line → comment keyword for a real deliverable.**
- Stays in the mindset lane. Matteo's story is the proof, never the topic.
- Facts only; cite something real (study, rule, legend) or leave it out.
- The keyword CTA needs the deliverable built before posting.
