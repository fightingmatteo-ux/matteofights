**The borrowed-credibility move (Soviet reel), beat by beat**
1. **Hook = the legend's name + a secret:** "Advanced training methods of Soviet boxers." The name does the selling, no face, no intro.
2. **Proof = archival footage:** 1990 VHS clips with the date stamp left visible. The grain and timestamp *are* the credibility.
3. **Contrast with what you do:** "didn't focus only on muscle strength… instead of mindlessly hitting the heavy bag."
4. **Mechanism in plain words:** "developing the nervous system… respond faster than the opponent could make a decision."
5. **Payoff + keyword CTA:** "Comment SOVIET" for a protocol based on the method (lead magnet).
- Format: voiceover only, letterboxed footage, small all-caps caption line above each clip, 1 line per 2–3s.

**Pattern across all three**
- Every reel ends on a **comment-keyword → free thing** CTA. The one with the strongest offer got 322 comments on 501 likes.

## Why the technique works (for @matteofights)
- **Borrowed authority.** "Tyson did this" is proof the viewer already accepts. Matteo doesn't have
  to be the authority, the legend is.
- **Name recognition stops the scroll.** A famous name in the hook pattern-interrupts faster than an
  abstract benefit.
- **Comment bait.** People argue about legends ("Ali > Tyson"). Arguments are engagement signal.
- **Fits both proven archetypes.** Each list item gets a legend attached:
  - Tactical (saves): "#3. Lomachenko's angles 👣 ..."
  - Identity (shares): "#5. You study film like Mayweather ..."

## Rules when using legends
- **Facts only.** Only attach a habit/technique that's well documented. A wrong claim gets torn
  apart in the comments and kills credibility.
- **Legend = proof, Matteo = coach.** The legend proves it works. The line still talks to the viewer
  in second person ("you"), per `context/voice.md`.
- **Mix eras + sports.** Boxing and MMA/Muay Thai legends widen reach.
